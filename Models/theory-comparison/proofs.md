# Theory Comparison Proofs

All results below use only the axioms and rules of this model.

## Proposition 1: Declared Theories Are Structurally Comparable

Two theories with declared components can be compared component by component.

### Proof

By Axiom 1, each theory has components `V`, `A`, `C`, `R`, and `Q`. Rule 1 gives a comparison for each corresponding component. Therefore declared theories are structurally comparable. QED.

## Proposition 2: Conditions Can Be Compared Without Being Identical

A comparison of conditions does not require the conditions to have the same meaning.

### Proof

By Axiom 2:

`C(T) ⊗ C(H) ↓ Compare(C(T), C(H))`

The axiom limits this comparison to form unless a semantic translation is supplied. Therefore comparison is possible without identity of meaning. QED.

## Proposition 3: Shared Form Does Not Establish Soundness

Structural resemblance between theories does not prove either theory sound.

### Proof

Axiom 3 permits recognition of a shared form. Axiom 8 states that structural similarity does not infer soundness, consistency, truth, or empirical adequacy. Rule 7 prohibits such a collapse. Therefore shared form does not establish soundness. QED.

## Proposition 4: Incompatibility Is Domain-Relative

A detected incompatibility applies only to the translated comparison domain that produced it.

### Proof

Rule 6 records `In(T, H)` when translated conditions or rules assign structurally distinct results for a selected comparison domain. The rule does not apply the result to every component. Therefore incompatibility is domain-relative. QED.
