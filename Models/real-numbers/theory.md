# Real Numbers Model

**Status:** Foundational model
**Role:** Defines real numbers as completed approximation structures.

## Purpose

This model extends finite arithmetic into a theory of continuous magnitude. A real number is not identified with one finite decimal, one fraction, or one natural successor history. It is the stable value represented by an indefinitely refinable rational approximation structure.

The model defines rational approximation, convergence, equality of limits, order, and the operations of addition and multiplication on completed values. It does not define complex numbers, differentiation, integration, or a topology beyond what is required for convergence.

## Central Idea

A rational structure gives a finite exact relation. A real structure is a completion of a rational approximation sequence whose uncertainty can be made smaller than every positive rational bound.

`C → L(C)`

Here `C` is an approximation structure and `L(C)` is its completed limit reference. A real is therefore a limit structure, not a final member of an endless sequence.

## Relation to Other Models

The Natural Numbers Model provides the finite successor structures used to index approximations. This model assumes rational structures as finite ratios of integer structures and adds a completion principle that admits limits not themselves rational.
