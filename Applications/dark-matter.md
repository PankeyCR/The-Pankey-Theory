# Dark Matter Application Theory

**Status:** Application theory under development
**Role:** Provides a structural interpretation of the dark-matter phenomenon using The Pankey Language, the Logic and Illogic Model, and the Natural Numbers Model.

## Scope

This is an application theory, not a replacement for general relativity, quantum field theory, or an empirical particle model. Its purpose is to state one possible structural account of a phenomenon conventionally described as dark matter: a component inferred through gravitational effects that has no confirmed electromagnetic coupling.

The symbols used below receive their meaning from this theory. The underlying language supplies only their structural form.

## References

| Reference | Interpretation in this theory |
|---|---|
| `D` | A dark-matter structure. |
| `O` | An ordinary-matter structure. |
| `G` | A gravitational interaction. |
| `E` | An electromagnetic interaction. |
| `W` | A weak interaction. |
| `S` | A strong interaction. |
| `R` | An unresolved residue. |
| `V` | A gravitational manifestation path. |
| `C` | The condition that only gravitational manifestation is declared. |

## Structural Forms

The Logic and Illogic Model classifies a structure with a declared outcome as logical and a structure with a declared return as illogical. This application adopts that distinction.

Ordinary matter is represented as a structure with declared interaction outcomes:

`O ▷ (G ∧ E ∧ W ∧ S) ⊢ C_O`

Dark matter is represented as a hybrid structure:

`D ▷ G : V ⊢ C ∧ R ⟳ R ⊢ C`

The first component declares a gravitational outcome. The second component declares an unresolved return. The `∧` means that both components belong to the same dark-matter structure; it does not mean that they are physically identical.

## Axioms

### Axiom 1: Declared Gravitational Resolution

`D ▷ G : V ⊢ C`

Under condition `C`, a dark-matter structure has a declared gravitational manifestation through `V`.

### Axiom 2: Unresolved Residue

`R ⟳ R ⊢ C`

Under the same condition, the residue has no declared terminal resolution in this theory.

### Axiom 3: Selective Declaration

`C = G ∧ ¬E ∧ ¬W ∧ ¬S`

The condition declares gravity as an interaction outcome for `D`. It does not declare electromagnetic, weak, or strong outcomes. Negation here records the absence of a declared outcome in this theory; it is not a proof that those interactions are impossible in nature.

## Collapse Rule

The Natural Numbers Model permits a finite constructed structure to be reinterpreted by collapse when the theory supplies such a rule. This application defines one collapse:

`(D ⊗ G) ↓ G_D ⊢ C`

`G_D` is the gravitationally manifested representation of `D`. The rule does not collapse `R` into `E`, `W`, or `S`, because the condition `C` contains no such declared outcome.

## Consequences

1. `D` is logical with respect to its declared gravitational manifestation: `D ▷ G : V ⊢ C`.
2. `D` is illogical with respect to its unresolved residue: `R ⟳ R ⊢ C`.
3. Therefore, `D` is neither wholly logical nor wholly illogical under this application theory; it is a composed logic-illogic hybrid.
4. The structure parallels the Natural Numbers Model: each finite natural-number construction has a declared collapse, while the total successor return has no final member. Likewise, `D` has a declared gravitational aspect while `R` remains non-terminal under `C`.

## Interpretation

This theory interprets dark matter as a structure whose gravitational effect is resolved while part of its interaction structure remains unresolved. It does not identify unresolved structure with mass, nor does it derive a particle mass, density profile, lensing curve, or cosmological abundance.

Its empirical content is conditional: the theory is useful only if the declared gravitational manifestation can be translated into quantitative predictions that agree with observation and distinguish it from existing dark-matter models.

## Open Requirements

To develop this application theory further, it needs:

- a definition of the path `V` in terms of measurable gravitational quantities;
- a translation from `G_D` to a general-relativistic or other established physical representation;
- explicit conditions for any non-gravitational interaction that is later observed;
- quantitative predictions that could confirm or falsify the interpretation.
